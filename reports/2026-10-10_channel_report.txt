0. HOW TO READ THIS REPORT

2.1 FIBONACCI SPIRAL: "The golden ratio hides in this spiral ✨"   (THE WINNER)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FACTS
- Views 31,530 (28,692 of them in the last 48 hours); +20 subscribers.
- Stayed to watch 69.5% (69.3% stayed / 30.7% swiped).
- AVD 0:19 on an 18 s video: on average people watched MORE than the whole video (rewatches).
- Engaged views 16K, unique viewers 4.4K, watch time 91.8 hours.
  (Unique viewers in Studio lag behind and are often under-counted. Even so, engaged views far above
  unique viewers means many people looped it 2-4 times.)
- Traffic: Shorts feed 92.9%, YouTube search 5.1%, other YouTube features 1.3%, browse 0.3%,
  channel pages 0.3%. Top search term: "golden ratio" (9.6% of search views).
- Views curve: slow on day 1 (about 5k), then accelerating on day 2 (to about 27k), still rising.
- Audience: male, 25-34 49.4%, 13-17 27.4%, 18-24 23.2%. Devices: phone 78.7%, tablet 9.5%,
  computer 8.3%, TV 3.3%. Countries VERY spread: the top one (US) is only 0.9%, then the
  Philippines 0.8%, Russia 0.5%, India 0.5%, Australia 0.4%.
- Only 0.2% of watch time came from subscribers: almost everyone is new.

WHAT HAPPENED
- It passed the first test with ~70% STW, so YouTube kept expanding it in waves. That's why the
  curve gets steeper on day 2 instead of flattening.

WHY (opinion)
1. A famous name. "Golden ratio" and "Fibonacci" are known worldwide and actually searched (it is
   the only video with real search traffic). People recognise them in under a second.
2. The first frame states the game. It is almost empty: one dot, "F(1) = 1" and
   "ratio = 1.0000000000". The brain immediately expects "this will grow, and that number will
   settle on something". That expectation is the reason not to swipe.
3. Something changes every half-second. A new square, a bigger F(n), and the ratio digits move.
   There is never a dull moment.
4. A visible finish line. The digits turn gold one by one toward 1.6180339887. People wait for the
   last gold digit; that's the payoff.
5. Short (18 s) plus a smooth loop: the rewind-dive back to "1" makes people watch again, and
   rewatches are a very strong signal.
6. A clean, readable picture: big squares, big numbers, high contrast; easy to read on a phone.
7. Universal: numbers and shapes need no language, so YouTube could test it in every country.
   That's why the country list is so flat.

LESSON
This is your template: a famous constant or name, a first frame that states the game with a number,
a counter that changes constantly, a visible finish line, 15-20 s, and a loop.
